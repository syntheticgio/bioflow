From Reads to Biology

A field guide to genome assembly and downstream analysis

August 2026

Abstract

Modern sequencing projects generate resources — assemblies, annotations, variant call sets, expression matrices — with striking efficiency. Turning those resources into biological claims is a different problem, and it is the one most projects underestimate. This guide maps the full path from raw reads to defensible conclusions: the assembly pipeline and where its real risks sit; annotation as the load-bearing step that quietly determines the reliability of everything downstream; a ten-rung analysis ladder from orthology to generative models; the exemplar multi-track workflows that recent landmark papers actually ran; and an honest accounting of where the methods do not deliver what their abstracts promise.

The intended reader is comfortable with the vocabulary — knows what a BAM file is, has run BUSCO, can read a Nextflow config — but wants the conceptual scaffolding and current tool landscape in one place, with citations. Software versions and community defaults reflect the state of the field as of August 2026 and are cited so they can be re-checked.

Five workflow diagrams accompany this guide and are referenced as Figures 1–5.

1. Introduction: the gap between a pipeline and a result

1.1 The problem, stated plainly

A typical genome paper's bioinformatics section reduces to two chains:

reads → [hifiasm/Flye] → contigs → [Hi-C] → chromosomes → [BRAKER/MAKER] → gene models
                                                                  ↓
reads → [STAR] → counts → [DESeq2] → DE genes → [clusterProfiler] → GO/KEGG terms

Everything up to the final arrow is resource generation. The enriched GO term list at the end is not an answer; it is an input to reasoning that has not happened yet. The difference between a forgettable genome paper and a landmark one lies almost entirely in what occurs after these standard outputs exist.

Two findings from the recent literature sharpen this point, and both are worth internalising before anything else.

Enrichment analysis is more fragile than its ubiquity suggests. Over-representation analysis run against a whole-genome background, without correcting for gene-length and expression bias, will return statistically significant GO terms from randomly drawn gene lists. GOAT (Koopmans, Communications Biology, 2024, 10.1038/s42003-024-06454-5) was developed specifically because standard ORA and GSEA null models are miscalibrated. clusterProfiler and g:Profiler are good software; the failure mode is methodological, not implementational. If a paper's central biological claim rests on a GO barplot, that claim is weakly supported.

Annotation quality dominates comparative results. A 2025 analysis in Bioinformatics (41(7):btaf365, 10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf365) demonstrated that whether your GFF files are internally consistent matters substantially more than which orthology inference algorithm you choose. The corollary arrives from the opposite direction: different orthology algorithms converge on similar orthogroups when applied to consistently annotated Brassicaceae genomes (2025, PMID 39906489). The practical instruction is unusual but clear — stop tool-shopping for orthology, and spend that effort on annotation consistency instead.

1.2 Three eras, and why the current one is different

It helps to place current practice historically, because much of the received wisdom in circulation was formed under constraints that no longer apply.

The short-read era (roughly 2008–2015) produced assemblies from 100–150 bp reads. Repeats longer than the insert size were unresolvable, so they collapsed; heterozygous regions produced tangled graphs that assemblers resolved by picking one allele arbitrarily. Scaffold N50 in the tens or hundreds of kilobases was respectable. Almost every methodological habit that still irritates — mandatory polishing, aggressive haplotig purging, treating repeats purely as contamination — dates from here.

The long-read era (2015–2021) replaced short reads with PacBio and Oxford Nanopore reads of tens of kilobases. Single reads now spanned most repeats. The tradeoff was accuracy: early long reads carried 10–15% error, so assemblies required heavy polishing with short reads. PacBio HiFi changed this by circularising the template and sequencing it repeatedly, yielding reads that are both long (15–25 kb) and accurate (Q30+). Once reads were long and accurate, the assembly graph stopped being a tangle to resolve heuristically and became a structure that largely reflects the underlying genome.

The telomere-to-telomere era (2021–present) followed. T2T-CHM13 (Nurk et al., Science, 2022, 10.1126/science.abj6987) delivered the first gap-free human genome by combining HiFi with ultra-long ONT reads that span even satellite arrays. The methodological shift matters more than the artifact: assembly became an engineering discipline with per-chromosome completion tracking, and the hardest 8% of the genome — centromeres, acrocentric short arms, rDNA arrays — became a research subject rather than a masked region.

The consequence for anyone planning a project today is that the bottleneck has moved. It is no longer assembly contiguity. It is annotation quality, coordinate stability, and the interpretive steps above them.

PART I · WHAT THE ASSEMBLY LADDER BUYS YOU

2. Contiguity, certainty, and four orthogonal axes

2.1 The ladder is about contiguity, and it is paid for with certainty

The familiar progression is:

reads      every base observed directly, short range
  ↓        overlap / graph assembly
contigs    gapless — every base still observed
  ↓        Hi-C / linkage map / optical map
scaffolds  ordered + oriented, with N-gaps of ESTIMATED length
  ↓        anchoring to karyotype or reference
chromosomes  pseudomolecules — the joins are inference, not observation

It is natural to read this as increasing resolution. It is closer to the opposite.

A contig is a claim that you observed every base within it. A scaffold is that claim plus a weaker one: an assertion about order, orientation, and an approximate gap size that you did not observe. A chromosome-scale scaffold adds a further assertion that a particular set of scaffolds belongs together, derived from a probabilistic contact signal rather than from any single read spanning the join.

Base-level resolution therefore decreases as you climb. Only span increases. This is why a paper reporting scaffold N50 alone is concealing something, and why assembly QC needs more than one number.

Understanding why Hi-C can do this is worth a paragraph, because it explains the failure modes. Hi-C crosslinks chromatin in situ, digests it, ligates the crosslinked fragments, and sequences the junctions. Because sequences that are physically close in the nucleus ligate more often, and because physical proximity correlates strongly with linear distance along a chromosome, the frequency of read pairs joining two contigs is a proxy for how close they are on the same chromosome. It is a statistical signal, not a measurement — which is exactly why the contact map must be inspected by a human before the scaffolds are trusted, and why a misjoin can look perfectly plausible to an automated scaffolder.

2.2 Four orthogonal axes, not one ladder

Axis Question Measured by
Contiguity How long are the pieces? contig N50, scaffold N50, gap count, LAI
Correctness Are the bases and the joins right? Merqury/MERQURY.FK QV, CRAQ, Inspector, GCI, contact-map inspection
Completeness Is anything missing? BUSCO/compleasm, k-mer completeness, span vs C-value
Phasing Which haplotype is this? hap-mer blob plots, switch error rate

These are genuinely independent. An assembly can be highly contiguous and structurally wrong; complete and badly phased; correct at base level and missing 15% of the gene space. Reporting only one axis is the most common form of quiet overclaiming in genome papers.

2.3 Phasing: the axis most often omitted

In a diploid organism, every locus exists in two versions. An assembler that does not phase will, at each heterozygous site, either pick one allele or produce two divergent contigs representing the same locus (haplotigs). Both outcomes are lossy — the first averages two genomes into a chimera that matches neither, the second inflates assembly span and duplicates gene content.

The phasing axis has its own ladder: collapsed consensus → primary/alternate (haplotigs assigned arbitrarily) → pseudo-haplotypes → fully phased diploid → phased telomere-to-telomere. Climbing this ladder does genuinely increase resolution, because you stop averaging two different genomes together. For a heterozygous outcrossing plant, this is arguably the more important axis, and it is routinely unreported.

2.4 Why the staged pipeline has partly collapsed

The reads → contigs → scaffolds → chromosomes sequence is a legacy of the short-read era, when assembly, scaffolding and phasing were necessarily separate tools operating on finished sequence.

That separation is no longer required. hifiasm --h1/--h2 phases and scaffolds inside the assembly graph in a single pass. Verkko 2 (Antipov et al., Genome Research, 2025, 10.1101/gr.280383.124) integrates Hi-C and Pore-C data directly into its multiplex De Bruijn graph, using proximity ligation for both phasing and scaffolding rather than as a post-hoc step.

The reason graph-integrated approaches win is specific and worth stating: the graph still knows what is ambiguous. A post-hoc scaffolder receives only resolved sequence and must infer what was collapsed, guessing at information the assembler already had and discarded.

Figure 1 · The assembly pipeline as loops. Phases 0–10 with the software at each step. The dashed back-edges are the substance: quality accrues from re-entering a phase, not from advancing past it. Loops 1–3 purchase contiguity by spending certainty; Loop 4 is the only place certainty is measured.

2.5 Where "successively better" is genuinely right

Progress happens within stages, as refinement loops rather than along a linear staircase (Figure 1):

assemble → map reads back → polish → purge haplotigs → re-assess
        → gap-fill → manually curate the contact map → re-assess

Most of the final quality comes from that loop. This is why the phase gates in Section 3 matter more than the phase order.

2.6 A consequence for very large, heterozygous genomes

The published assembly of Lilium sargentiae (Nature Communications, 2025, 10.1038/s41467-024-55545-8) is the clearest illustration. Its final assembly reports contig N50 of roughly 0.93 Mb against scaffold N50 of 2.85 Gb — a jump of about 3,000×. Essentially all of the "chromosome-ness" is Hi-C inference layered over comparatively short contigs.

Expect the same lopsided shape for any repeat-rich genome above ~10 Gb, and note where it puts the risk: not in assembly, but in many thousands of Hi-C joins that no read spans. Three practical consequences follow. Use a scaffolder tolerant of low contig N50 and shallow Hi-C depth (Section 3.7). Treat manual contact-map curation as mandatory, since it is the only step that inspects those joins directly. And treat any karyotype expectation as a hypothesis to test rather than a target to satisfy — forcing scaffolds to reach a predetermined chromosome count is how genuine chromosomal variation gets assembled away.

3.1 Phase 0 — Prior knowledge

This is the cheapest phase and the one most often skipped, which is unfortunate because information gathered here determines parameters, provides validation targets, and occasionally reveals that the project has already been done.

Inventory: close-relative assemblies, cytogenetic data (chromosome number, karyotype formula), genome size estimates, ploidy, organellar genomes, existing transcriptomes, genetic linkage maps, molecular markers, FISH mapping, and optical maps. Then document what is missing and how you will work around each gap.

When a close relative exists:

minimap2 -x asm5 -t 32 reference.fasta your_assembly.fasta > alignment.paf
ragtag.py scaffold reference.fasta your_assembly.fasta -t 32 -o ragtag_out
nucmer --mum -t 32 reference.fasta your_assembly.fasta -p nucmer_out
mummerplot --png --layout -p nucmer_out nucmer_out.delta
liftoff -g reference.gff3 -o transferred.gff3 your_assembly.fasta reference.fasta

Reference-guided scaffolding imports the reference's errors and forces incorrect joins across genuine rearrangements. Use RagTag to validate and refine a de novo plus Hi-C assembly, never as the primary scaffolder, and treat the Hi-C contact map as the tiebreaker when the two disagree. Adding SyRI after nucmer converts the synteny picture into an explicit rearrangement callset, which is more useful than a dotplot for deciding whether a discordance is real.

A practical caution on published prior-knowledge documents and review tables: accession numbers and transcriptome citations propagate errors readily, and a wrong accession can silently derail organellar filtering or annotation transfer. Resolve every accession against the primary record (NCBI Nucleotide, ENA, or GWH) rather than inheriting it from a secondary source.

3.2 Phase 1 — Data acquisition

prefetch SRR12345678 --max-size 500G --output-directory /path/to/data

# --temp MUST be on the same filesystem as the output
fasterq-dump --threads 8 --temp /same/filesystem/tmp SRR12345678.sra

# No built-in compression in the current toolkit — pipe to pigz:
fasterq-dump --threads 8 --temp /same/filesystem/tmp --concatenate-reads \
  SRR12345678.sra -Z | pigz -p 8 > SRR12345678.fastq.gz

Three operational notes save real time. -Z/--stdout works only with --split-spot, --concatenate-reads and --fasta-unsorted — it is incompatible with --split-3 and --split-files, so any recipe combining -Z with split output is broken. NCBI's own guidance is to budget roughly 7× the accession size for output plus about 6× for temporary space; the toolkit wiki suggests 8–10× total, and the most common failure is a --temp directory pointed at a smaller filesystem than the output. --size-check off, --disk-limit and --disk-limit-tmp are the escape hatches when fasterq-dump aborts on a perceived space shortage.

Housekeeping: delete .sra files only after the FASTQ verifies (gzip -t plus a read-count check), and never delete FASTQ until the project is complete, since re-downloads take days at these volumes.

3.3 Phase 2 — QC, ploidy, and genome estimation

# Long-read QC — cramino reads BAM/CRAM natively
cramino --threads 8 reads.bam
seqkit stats -a reads.fq.gz              # quick alternative

# k-mer counting
jellyfish count -C -m 21 -s 20G -t 32 reads.fq.gz -o reads.jf
jellyfish histo -t 32 reads.jf > reads.histo

# PLOIDY FIRST — do not assume diploid
smudgeplot ...                            # or PloidyPlot from the FastK suite

# Then model, with the ploidy you measured
genomescope2 -i reads.histo -o genomescope_out -k 21 -p <measured_ploidy>

What a k-mer spectrum tells you, and why it matters. Count every distinct k-mer in the reads and histogram their multiplicities. A homozygous diploid genome sequenced at 40× produces one peak at 40. A heterozygous diploid produces two: a heterozygous peak near 20 (k-mers present on one haplotype only) and a homozygous peak near 40. Peak positions give coverage, peak areas give genome size, the ratio of the two peaks gives heterozygosity, and the long right tail is repeat content. GenomeScope2 fits this model formally. This is why the step is worth doing before assembly: it predicts how much trouble you are in.

Several parameter choices here are load-bearing:

  • NanoStat is deprecated — its own README directs users to Cramino and states it will receive no further updates. It is also ONT-oriented and cannot filter on the HiFi rq tag, which means the useful step of filtering to rq ≥ 0.99 is unavailable through it.
  • jellyfish -s is an estimate of the number of distinct k-mers to store, not a size in bases. Jellyfish 2 handles undersizing by growing the hash or spilling to disk and merging, so the consequence is slowness rather than failure.
  • Hardcoding -p 2 is the single most consequential wrong default in a plant pipeline. Measure ploidy with smudgeplot or PloidyPlot first; polyploidy is common and silently invalidates every downstream size estimate.
  • k=21 is tuned for genomes of roughly 3 Gb. For much larger genomes it is wrong, and the error propagates through Jellyfish, GenomeScope, meryl and Merqury alike. Merqury ships best_k.sh <genome_size>; use it and reuse the result consistently.
  • Above roughly 10 Gb, replace the Jellyfish stack with FastK. FastK is disk-based with bounded memory; its documentation cites a 32 Gb axolotl genome (3.2 Tbp of data) counted in 12 GB of RAM, at the cost of roughly 6.5 TB of scratch disk. Pair it with GeneScopeFK and MerquryFK.

Gate: ploidy measured rather than assumed, and the GenomeScope estimate consistent with prior knowledge or the discrepancy explained.

3.4 Phase 3 — Organellar separation

Chloroplast DNA is present at 50–100 copies per cell and can constitute 10–20% of reads from leaf tissue. Its extreme coverage distorts k-mer spectra and consumes assembler attention.

minimap2 -t 32 -ax map-hifi chloroplast_ref.fasta hifi.fq.gz | \
  samtools fastq -f 4 - > hifi_nuclear.fq

# If no organellar reference exists (requires short reads):
get_organelle_from_reads.py -1 R1.fastq.gz -2 R2.fastq.gz \
  -o chloroplast_out -F embplant_pt -R 15 -t 16

Two cautions. Discarding everything that maps also discards reads from NUMTs and NUPTs — nuclear insertions of organellar DNA, which are real genomic features. Filter on alignment coverage (require, say, 90% of the read aligned) rather than any hit. Alternatively, skip read-level depletion entirely: modern assemblers handle organelles competently, and you can identify and remove the high-coverage circular contigs afterwards.

Keep the organellar assemblies. Chloroplast genomes are publishable in their own right and are the standard substrate for plant phylogenetics. Identifying organellar contigs in the final assembly by coverage, circularity and homology also yields NUMT/NUPT annotation essentially for free.

3.5 Phase 4 — Primary long-read assembly

# hifiasm 0.25.0
hifiasm -o assembly -t 32 --telo-m CCCTAAA hifi_nuclear.fq.gz 2> hifiasm.log

# Hi-C integrated — usually better than post-hoc scaffolding
hifiasm -o assembly -t 32 --h1 hic_R1.fq.gz --h2 hic_R2.fq.gz hifi.fq.gz

# Very large genome — reduce k-mer counting memory
hifiasm -o assembly -t 32 -f38 hifi.fq.gz

Memory planning. Published benchmarks from the hifiasm authors:

Genome Size hifiasm RAM Flye RAM
A. thaliana 0.12 Gb — 59 GB
Strawberry 0.8 Gb 91 GB —
Maize B73 2.2 Gb 68 GB —

Two structural points follow. Memory tracks total input bases, not genome size — strawberry at 0.8 Gb and 36× coverage used more RAM than maize at 2.2 Gb and 22×. Any per-gigabase rule of thumb is unsound unless it also states the coverage. And Flye is not a low-memory fallback: on identical human HiFi data it used more memory than hifiasm. When hifiasm exceeds available RAM, the real levers are -f38/-f39 (which reduce k-mer counting memory) and a larger machine.

Assembler notes: flye --pacbio-hifi no longer requires --genome-size (optional since 2.8, needed only with --asm-coverage). Canu 2.x uses -pacbio for CLR and -pacbio-hifi for HiFi; for a very large HiFi genome Canu is not a realistic choice on runtime grounds.

Haplotig purging. Heterozygous genomes often retain duplicated haplotigs, visible as assembly span well above the estimate and elevated BUSCO duplication:

# Read-to-assembly alignment feeds pbcstat
minimap2 -x map-hifi -t 32 primary.fasta hifi.fq.gz | gzip -c > reads.paf.gz
pbcstat reads.paf.gz
calcuts PB.stat > cutoffs 2> calcuts.log

# Self-alignment — asm5 is correct here
split_fa primary.fasta > primary.split.fa
minimap2 -x asm5 -DP primary.split.fa primary.split.fa | gzip -c > split.self.paf.gz
purge_dups -2 -T cutoffs -c PB.base.cov split.self.paf.gz > dups.bed
get_seqs -e dups.bed primary.fasta

Note that pbcstat, calcuts and PB.base.cov require the read-alignment PAF, not the self-alignment PAF — a common and silent misconfiguration. Also note that hifiasm applies -l3 purging by default; running purge_dups again on default output frequently over-purges. It is primarily useful for -l0 runs and for Flye or Canu output.

A related trap specific to plants: ancient whole-genome duplication leaves paralogous blocks at 80–98% identity, which look very much like haplotigs. Be conservative with purging on genomes with recent WGD history, and check BUSCO before and after.

Gate: span within ±15% of the estimate or the discrepancy explained; contig N50 in the expected range. For very large heterozygous genomes, expect a low contig N50 (Section 2.6).

3.6 Phase 5 — Polishing

For a pure HiFi assembly from hifiasm, polishing is usually unnecessary and frequently harmful. Base accuracy is already QV 50–60, and naive short-read polishing reduces QV by collapsing heterozygous sites toward one allele. That failure mode is precisely why merfin exists.

# For HiFi, NextPolish2 supersedes NextPolish (its own README redirects HiFi users)

# merfin — scores and filters variant calls against a k-mer model
meryl count k=<best_k> output reads.meryl reads.fq.gz
merfin -polish \
  -sequence assembly.fasta \
  -readmers reads.meryl \
  -prob lookup_table.txt \
  -vcf calls.vcf.gz \
  -output merfin_polished

Three merfin details are easy to get wrong: -polish mode requires a -vcf, since it filters variant calls rather than performing alignment-based correction; -seqmers refers to the assembly's k-mer database, not the reads' (it is auto-generated from -sequence if omitted); and merfin consumes meryl databases, not Jellyfish .jf files. Prefer -prob with a lookup table over a bare -peak.

DeepConsensus does not apply to Revio data. It operates on subreads, and Revio does not retain them — HiFi consensus is computed on-instrument. It remains relevant only to legacy Sequel II/IIe subread BAMs.

3.7 Phase 6 — Chromosome-scale scaffolding

samtools faidx polished.fasta               # YaHS requires the .fai
bwa index polished.fasta

bwa mem -5SP -t 32 polished.fasta hic_R1.fastq.gz hic_R2.fastq.gz | \
  samtools sort -@32 -n -o hic_namesorted.bam

# Rigorous QC route (4DN)
cut -f1,2 polished.fasta.fai > polished.genome
pairtools parse --min-mapq 40 --walks-policy 5unique --max-inter-align-gap 30 \
  --nproc-in 16 --nproc-out 16 --chroms-path polished.genome hic_namesorted.bam > parsed.pairsam
pairtools sort --nproc 16 parsed.pairsam > parsed.sorted.pairsam
pairtools dedup parsed.sorted.pairsam > dedup.pairsam
pairtools split --output-pairs dedup.pairs dedup.pairsam

# Scaffold
haphic pipeline polished.fasta hic_aligned.bam <n_chromosomes> --threads 32
# or
yahs -o yahs_out polished.fasta hic_aligned.bam

The bwa mem -5SP flag combination is not optional and is worth understanding: -5 marks the split alignment with the shortest coordinate as primary, -S skips mate rescue, and -P skips pairing. Together they prevent bwa from applying shotgun insert-size assumptions, which are meaningless for Hi-C where the "insert" spans a ligation junction. Do not add -M.

HapHiC is the current default for plant genomes (Zeng et al., Nature Plants 10:1184–1200, 2024, 10.1038/s41477-024-01755-3). It is reference-independent — the decisive argument against ALLHiC for a novel genome — combines an efficiency-improved 3D-DNA iterative algorithm with ALLHiC's allele-aware optimisation, and is explicitly tolerant of low contig N50, shallow Hi-C depth and assembly errors. Those are exactly the conditions of a large heterozygous plant assembly. It is not on bioconda; install from source. YaHS remains a reasonable default for cleaner assemblies; 3D-DNA and ALLHiC are now legacy, the latter retained mainly for autopolyploids where a close reference exists. Chromap is a faster aligner than bwa for very large genomes and is supported by HapHiC.

Manual curation is not optional. Inspect the contact map in PretextView or Juicebox before accepting scaffolds, looking for misjoins (off-diagonal blocks within a chromosome), inversions (bowtie patterns), and contigs belonging to no chromosome (leave those unplaced rather than forcing them). Automated scaffolders reliably produce a handful of misjoins on polyploid and repeat-rich genomes.

Gate: scaffold count consistent with the karyotype expectation, contact map clean after curation. If the count differs, investigate rather than force — the candidate explanations are a wrong ploidy assumption, mis-scaffolding, or genuine chromosome-number variation in the accession you sequenced.

3.8 Phase 7 — Contamination and adaptor screening

PART III · ANNOTATION

4. Annotation is a fan-in, not a step

4.1 Why annotation is load-bearing

Annotation occupies an unusual position: it is the last step of resource generation and the first input to every interpretive analysis. Orthology inference, gene family expansion tests, selection analysis, presence/absence variation, differential expression, and pangenome graph interpretation all consume gene models. An error introduced here does not stay local — it propagates into every downstream conclusion, and because all downstream analyses inherit the same error, they will agree with each other. Mutual agreement among analyses sharing a corrupted input reads, misleadingly, as corroboration.

This is the mechanism behind the finding cited in Section 1.1, and it recurs in a more dramatic form in pangenomics (Section 6.4).

Combiners resolve conflicts by evidence weight, so tool choice matters far less than what you feed them — and a QC failure is almost always answered by adding evidence, not by switching predictor.

The most useful way to think about annotation is as a ranking problem rather than a plumbing problem (Figure 3). Combiners resolve conflicts by evidence weight, so what you feed them matters more than which one you pick.

4.4 Annotation QC has three legs

Reporting only a BUSCO score is 2019-era practice. Current QC covers three orthogonal questions plus format integrity:

psauron -i proteins.fa -o psauron_out           # plausibility: do they look like proteins?
agat_sp_statistics.pl --gff annotation.gff3     # integrity: will anything parse this?

When OMArk flags fragmented or spurious models, the productive response is almost never to try a different predictor. It is to add evidence — Iso-Seq, or a closer relative to lift from.

4.5 Isoform-level annotation

Short-read and ab initio annotation systematically produces one model per locus. Plants exhibit pervasive intron retention and alternative splicing, and in many cases the splicing pattern is the phenotype — stress response and flowering time are the canonical examples. An annotation without isoforms cannot support splicing QTL analysis, cannot validate UTRs, and biases every downstream quantification.

The chain is: PacBio Iso-Seq or ONT cDNA/direct-RNA → IsoQuant, StringTie3 or Bambu → SQANTI3 for structural categorisation (full-splice-match, incomplete-splice-match, novel-in-catalog, novel-not-in-catalog) and artifact filtering, including RT-switching and intra-priming detection → TAMA to collapse and merge → integration into the structural annotation.

The relevant benchmark is Davis et al. (2026), "Benchmarking methods for genome annotation using nanopore direct RNA in a non-model crop plant," Bioinformatics Advances 6(1):vbag030, 10.1093/bioadv/vbag030. Working in barley, they found StringTie3 (89% precision) and IsoQuant (90%) the strongest reference-guided performers, with Bambu highest in precision (95%) but lower in sensitivity; both leading tools recovered more than 700 previously unannotated transcripts. That last number is the argument for doing this at all.

5.1 Rung 2 — Annotation as analysis

Gene-model prediction is resource generation; the analysis value appears when annotation is treated as comparative evidence.

The instructive move is methodological consistency. The 200-insect BRAKER study (Saenko et al., Scientific Data, 2026, 10.1038/s41597-026-06840-0) ran one pipeline across 200 genomes precisely so that gene-content comparisons would be apples-to-apples. When annotation methods differ across the genomes being compared, observed differences in gene content reflect the annotation pipeline as much as the biology. Consistency of methodology becomes the scientific instrument — this is the quiet backbone of comparative genomics, and it is why the finding in Section 1.1 is so consequential.

5.2 Rung 3 — Comparative genomics

This is the most common downstream layer in genome papers, and it has a standard skeleton that answers real questions:

  1. Orthology. OrthoFinder or eggNOG-mapper define gene families across species. What is conserved, and what is lineage-specific? The distinction that makes this hard is orthologs (separated by speciation) versus paralogs (separated by duplication) — only the former are the "same gene" in different species, and distinguishing them requires phylogenetic rather than purely similarity-based reasoning.
  2. Gene family dynamics. CAFE5 models birth-and-death rates along the phylogeny. Which families expanded in this lineage, and does that expansion track the phenotype?
  3. Selection. HyPhy and aBSREL compute dN/dS across branches and sites. Was duplication followed by adaptive neofunctionalisation, or by relaxed constraint and pseudogenisation?
  4. Synteny and whole-genome duplication. MCScanX, WGDI and JCVI identify collinear blocks and Ks distributions. What is this genome's duplication history?

Why Ks distributions date duplications. Synonymous substitutions do not change the encoded protein and are therefore approximately neutral, accumulating at a roughly clock-like rate. Plot Ks across all paralog pairs and a whole-genome duplication appears as a peak: every gene pair created at that moment has had the same time to diverge. Multiple peaks indicate multiple rounds.

The recurring analytical move across all four steps is family → expansion → selection → expression → phenotype. Nearly every plant genome paper that proposes a candidate gene for a trait runs this exact ladder.

5.3 Rung 4 — Pangenomics

The pattern: build many high-quality assemblies, construct a graph, and analyse gene presence/absence as a variable rather than a constant.

What a pangenome graph is. A linear reference forces every sample's sequence into coordinates defined by one individual; sequence absent from that individual is invisible, and sequence divergent from it maps poorly or not at all. A pangenome graph instead represents shared sequence as shared nodes and variation as alternative paths. Each genome is a walk through the graph. Reads map to the graph rather than to one arbitrary haplotype, which removes the reference bias that hides structural variants.

Two construction philosophies dominate. Minigraph-Cactus is reference-anchored: it starts from a designated reference, adds samples progressively, and partitions by chromosome. PGGB is reference-free: it performs all-versus-all alignment and induces the graph symmetrically, treating every genome as a first-class path.

The downstream analyses this enables:

  • Structural variant discovery by construction. Mapping short reads to a graph with vg giraffe rather than to a linear reference removes the bias that conceals SVs.
  • "The gene isn't in the reference" becomes findable. The bread wheat pangenome (Montenegro et al., Plant Journal, 2017) identified thousands of genes absent from the Chinese Spring reference.
  • Presence/absence as a phenotype. The peanut FAD2 study (Thompson et al., Nature Communications, 2025, 10.1038/s41467-025-67371-7) is the cleanest demonstration in the recent literature: a population-specific pangenome revealed a third FAD2 gene explaining the mid-oleic acid trait, and GWAS on the same data using single-reference markers missed the association entirely, because the causal gene is not in the reference. The graph was built with Minigraph-Cactus v2.6.7 using TR as reference; GWAS ran in TASSEL 5.0; the hits fall on chromosomes 09 and 19.

Builder choice is not neutral. The full text of the human pangenome paper is explicit about the tradeoffs: Minigraph records only structural variants ≥50 bp and produces graphs two orders of magnitude smaller in node count than base-level graphs; the PGGB graph contains roughly 5 Gb more sequence because it retains structurally divergent satellite regions the others omit; and Minigraph-Cactus is sensitive to input order and discards sequences that diverge too far from the reference — a limitation the literature flags as "especially problematic in high-diversity species." For a species complex with substantial chromosomal variation, that caveat determines the choice: PGGB's reference-free construction is likely correct, and demonstrating so would itself be a result.

Transposable elements must be handled at the pan level. panEDTA (Ou et al., Genome Research, 2024, 10.1101/gr.278131.123) annotates N genomes, builds a unified pan-TE library, and re-annotates all N. This is not optional polish. Per-genome TE libraries make TE content non-comparable across accessions, and a repeat family annotated in accession A but missed in accession B is indistinguishable from genuine presence/absence variation. For any multi-accession project, a unified library is a correctness requirement.

For evaluating builder choice on your own data, see "Toward a standardized framework for pangenome graph evaluation," GigaScience, 2025/26, 10.1093/gigascience/giaf121, and the review "Plant graph-based pangenomics: techniques, applications, and challenges," aBIOTECH, 2025, 10.1007/s42994-025-00206-7.

5.4 Rung 5 — Networks and integration

Once you have gene lists — differentially expressed genes, QTL intervals, family members — the question becomes how they act together.

WGCNA (Langfelder & Horvath, 2008) constructs weighted co-expression networks: cluster genes into modules by correlation, summarise each module by its eigengene, correlate module eigengenes with traits, and identify hub genes within trait-associated modules as candidates. The logic is that co-regulated genes are often functionally related, so a module correlating with a phenotype is a coherent hypothesis rather than a list.

MOFA+ (Argelaguet et al., Genome Biology, 2020) performs factor analysis across omics layers measured on the same samples, using automatic relevance determination priors to distinguish variation shared across modalities from variation confined to one. Factors become interpretable axes of coordinated multi-layer variation.

CellChat (Jin et al., Nature Communications, 2021) infers cell–cell communication from single-cell clusters via ligand–receptor pairs. Its most instructive feature is not the inference but the validation: the authors confirmed a predicted Edn3–Ednrb signal by RNAscope. Inference pipelines earn their keep when their predictions are checked.

The standard integration pattern is MOFA+ after clustering and WGCNA after differential expression; the network is the hypothesis generator, and the hubs are the validation targets.

Single-cell and spatial work in plants lags animal systems, for concrete reasons worth stating before designing such an experiment. The cell wall blocks standard droplet capture, forcing protoplasting — slow, biased toward easily released cell types, and inducing a stress transcriptome that confounds the biology under study. Single-nucleus RNA-seq avoids protoplasting stress but yields fewer transcripts with heavy intronic signal. Rigid tissue and high autofluorescence complicate imaging-based spatial methods. There are no plant cell-type marker atlases comparable to the Human Cell Atlas, so annotation is manual and irreproducible. And polyploidy with high paralog similarity breaks read assignment. See Nobori et al., New Phytologist, 2025, 10.1111/nph.70220.

The scvi-tools family (scVI and scANVI for integration, DestVI for spatial deconvolution, MultiVI for multiome), Squidpy for spatial neighbourhood statistics, and cell2location or RCTD for spot deconvolution complete the current toolkit.

5.5 Rung 6 — Association to causation

GWAS and QTL mapping produce intervals, not genes. Linkage disequilibrium means the associated marker is usually not the causal variant but merely correlated with it, and the interval may contain dozens of genes. The downstream ladder exists to narrow this.

  1. Fine-mapping. SuSiE-RSS (Zou, Carbonetto, Wang & Stephens, PLoS Genetics, 2022) frames fine-mapping as variable selection and returns credible sets — minimal sets of variants with high probability of containing the causal one. The original SuSiE is Wang, Sarkar, Carbonetto & Stephens, JRSS-B, 2020, 10.1111/rssb.12388; a SuSiE 2.0 preprint appeared in November 2025. PolyFun adds functional annotations as priors, upweighting variants in relevant regulatory contexts. A useful practical detail from the SuSiE-RSS paper: SuSiE-RSS, FINEMAP and DAP-G perform very similarly with in-sample LD, all degrade with out-of-sample LD, and SuSiE-RSS and DAP-G do not appear to require regularization while FINEMAP does. This matters if you fine-map against a reference LD panel rather than your own genotypes. Review: Li et al., Nature Reviews Genetics, 2025, 10.1038/s41576-025-00869-4.
  2. Colocalization. coloc (Giambartolomei et al., PLoS Genetics, 2014) asks whether a GWAS signal and an eQTL signal in the same region share a causal variant, or merely overlap by LD. It remains the single most-used bridge from "associated locus" to "regulatory mechanism."
  3. TWAS and Mendelian randomization. FUSION and S-PrediXcan impute genetically regulated expression and test it against the trait. Formal MR uses genetic variants as instrumental variables, with MR-Egger detecting directional pleiotropy through its intercept, plus weighted median, weighted mode, and MR-PRESSO for outlier removal — sensitivity analysis across estimators is mandatory rather than optional. MR-link-2 (Nature Communications, 2025, 10.1038/s41467-025-60868-1) is the current frontier for pleiotropy-robust cis-MR. The standing critique of TWAS is that its associations are not causal and are confounded by LD-driven co-regulation; coloc and TWAS answer different questions and are best run together.
  4. Polygenic scores. PRSice-2 (Choi & O'Reilly, GigaScience, 2019) and LDpred2 (Privé, Arbel & Vilhjálmsson, Bioinformatics, 2020, 10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa1029) aggregate genome-wide effects for prediction.
  5. In plants: QTL → candidate → validation. Fine-map the interval, often using bulked segregant analysis on an F2 population, which is dramatically cheaper than full GWAS. The FW7 strawberry study (Plant Genome, 2025) found a Fusarium wilt resistance gene masked by epistasis — a result BSA surfaced and standard GWAS would not have. Then triangulate with transcriptome and metabolome data, then validate.

Formal MR is rare in plants for structural reasons worth naming. Structured populations violate MR's independence assumptions, linkage blocks in inbred lines are enormous, and — decisively — you can simply make the knockout. In plants, transgenics and CRISPR are the causal-inference method; MR is a screening step at best.

One step is routinely skipped between discovery and association: SV genotyping. Discovery gives a variant set; you still must genotype it across the panel. Use kanpig for long reads (English et al., Nature Communications, 2025, 10.1038/s41467-025-58577-w) or vg giraffe against a pangenome graph for short reads, then merge and harmonise representations with Truvari (English et al., Genome Biology, 2022, 10.1186/s13059-022-02840-6). This is where most SV-GWAS attempts quietly fail — not at discovery, but at getting consistent genotypes across hundreds of samples.

AlphaGenome Nature, 2025/26, 10.1038/s41586-025-10014-0. Multi-modal — expression, splicing, accessibility, contact maps — at 1 Mb context, roughly one second per variant. API-only; weights not released.

The verdicts matter more than the models, and are covered in Section 7. In brief: these systems are useful for ranking the search space so that validation spends its budget where it counts. They do not replace validation, and the evidence that they beat well-tuned task-specific baselines is weaker than the surrounding discourse suggests.

5.7 Rung 8 — Epigenome and 3D genome from data you already have

This is the highest value-per-effort opportunity in a modern genome project, and it is widely missed.

HiFi and ONT reads already contain methylation calls. Both platforms detect base modifications directly from the raw signal — HiFi from kinetic information, ONT from current deviation — so a genome paper reporting an assembly but no methylome is discarding data it has already paid for. For PacBio, primrose has been superseded by jasmine (on-instrument on Revio), with pb-CpG-tools for per-site aggregation. For ONT, Dorado basecalling with modified-base models feeds modkit for pileup, bedMethyl output and differential methylation.

A plant-specific caution: some Dorado 5mC models report only CpG methylation. In plants, CHG and CHH methylation are biologically central — they are the primary contexts for transposon silencing and RNA-directed DNA methylation — so CpG-only calling discards most of the signal. Verify your model reports all three contexts before committing a run. Benchmark: "Comprehensive benchmarking of tools for nanopore-based detection of DNA methylation," bioRxiv 10.1101/2024.11.09.622763 (preprint). Minimod is a lighter alternative (bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.07.16.665072, preprint), and nf-core/methylong exists in development.

Hi-C reused for 3D genome architecture. The library used for scaffolding also yields A/B compartments, TAD-like domains and loops via pairtools → cooler → cooltools/FAN-C/HiCExplorer. An honest caveat: plant genomes largely lack the canonical CTCF-anchored TADs described in mammals — plants have no CTCF homolog — so A/B compartments and loops are the transferable concepts, and TADs mostly are not.

Chromatin accessibility via nf-core/atacseq and nf-core/chipseq completes the layer.

5.8 Rung 9 — Repeats as signal

Most pipelines treat repeats as an annotation nuisance to be masked. In a telomere-to-telomere assembly, centromere structure is a result.

Tool Purpose
TRASH Tandem Repeat Annotation and Structural Hierarchy — de novo satellite identification plus higher-order repeat (HOR) hierarchy; designed on plant T2T genomes (Bioinformatics 39(5):btad308, 2023).
HiCAT Automatic centromere annotation via hierarchical HOR decomposition.
StringDecomposer Decomposes centromeric sequence into monomer strings, upstream of HOR calling.
ULTRA / TRF Raw tandem-repeat finding; ULTRA is the modern probabilistic replacement for TRF.
tidk Telomeric repeat identification and quantification — per-chromosome-end telomere counts as a T2T completeness metric.

Why HORs matter. Centromeric satellite arrays are not random repeats; monomers are organised into higher-order repeat units that themselves repeat, and the HOR structure differs between species and often between chromosomes. Recovering it correctly is a strong indicator that the assembly resolved the centromere rather than collapsing it. CENH3 ChIP-seq is the functional assay that distinguishes an active centromere from a merely satellite-rich region.

TE annotation currency: EDTA v2.3.0 remains the plant workhorse for structural TE detection; TEtrimmer (Nature Communications, 2025, 10.1038/s41467-025-63889-y) automates the manual curation step — multiple sequence alignment extension, boundary definition, classification, interactive proofreading — and is reported to outperform both EDTA and RepeatModeler2; Earl Grey (MBE 41(4):msae068, 2024) offers an end-to-end alternative with iterative boundary refinement that addresses the chronic truncated-consensus problem. The 2026 plant default is EDTA for structural detection, TEtrimmer for curation, RepeatMasker with the curated library — or Earl Grey as a single command.

TE insertion polymorphism as a phenotype. GraffiTE (Nature Communications, 2024, 10.1038/s41467-024-53294-2) provides a unified graph-genome framework for discovering and genotyping TE insertion polymorphisms across assemblies and long reads. TEforest (Genetics, 2026, 10.1093/genetics/iyag101) trains on long-read assemblies to make short-read TE genotyping accurate, enabling cheap genotyping across hundreds of resequenced accessions.

Structural variation as a trait is well demonstrated in plants: Brassica napus — "Structural variation reshapes population gene expression and trait variation in 2,105 accessions," Nature Genetics, 2024, 10.1038/s41588-024-01957-7; and the tomato graph pangenome capturing missing heritability, Nature, 2022, 10.1038/s41586-022-04808-9.

5.9 Rung 10 — Orthogonal validation

Every rung above is computational. Rungs 1–9 can be internally consistent and collectively wrong, particularly since they share inputs. This rung concerns evidence that does not originate in a genome browser.

Proteogenomics for annotation validation. Search MS/MS spectra against a six-frame translation, or against the predicted proteome supplemented with unannotated-ORF decoys. Peptides mapping outside annotated coding sequence are direct physical evidence of missed genes, wrong exon boundaries, or wrong start codons. This is the only orthogonal validation of a gene model that is not itself computational. Public proteomics data exists in PRIDE for many crop species and is systematically underused.

Metabolomics as the readout for genome-predicted chemistry. GNPS molecular networking clusters MS/MS spectra by fragmentation similarity, grouping structurally related metabolites into molecular families even when individual compounds are unidentified.

The triangle to close. Genome papers commonly predict biosynthetic gene clusters and separately build co-expression networks, without joining them to the chemistry:

antiSMASH → candidate BGC
        ↓                    ↘
   WGCNA co-expression       GNPS molecular family
        ↓                    ↙
   co-regulated cluster genes ←→ candidate product
        ↓
   heterologous expression / CRISPR → proof

That triangle is the genome-to-metabolite workflow, and the edges between corners are where the biology is established. antiSMASH is at version 8.0 (Blin et al., Nucleic Acids Research 53(W1):W32–W38, 2025, 10.1093/nar/gkaf334).

Figure 5 · Downstream workflows trade outputs. Four programmes can all begin once an annotation exists, so a schedule shows them as independent. They are not: association analysis is weak without the pangenome's structural variant matrix, and the chemistry track is guesswork without a validated locus. The cross-edges belong to no single workstream, which is why they are the parts most often left undone.

6. What landmark papers actually ran

Tool traces below were extracted from published full text, in order of first appearance (Figures 1–5 render these graphically).

6.1 T2T human — assembly as an engineering discipline

Nurk et al., Science, 2022; Verkko 2, Antipov et al., Genome Research, 2025

HiFi (hifiasm; trio-phased)  ─┐
ONT ultralong ───────────────┼→ multiplex De Bruijn graph → haplotype paths (Rukki)
Hi-C / Pore-C ───────────────┘      → Hi-C scaffolding → consensus → T2T contigs
   ↓
BUSCO + Merqury + CRAQ validation → annotation via CAT + Liftoff (GENCODE v35 projection)
   ↓
repeat-resolution analysis (rDNA morph graphs; satellite arrays) → biology of the hardest 8%

Three moves generalise. First, graph assembly with per-chromosome completion tracking: Verkko 2 reports an average of 39 of 46 chromosomes assembled as scaffolds across 19 human genomes, with 21 of those as gapless contigs, and identifies the acrocentric short arms and their rDNA arrays as the explicit remaining problem. Second, annotation by transfer and refinement rather than de novo prediction alone — the published method combines the CAT annotation with genes identified only by Liftoff. Third, the rDNA morph graph, a custom graph structure built specifically for the 5S/45S arrays, is a model of building a tool for one question rather than forcing the question into an existing tool.

6.2 The plant chromosome-scale meta-pipeline

Populus deltoides, BMC Biology, 2025; Lablab purpureus T2T, Scientific Data, 2025; cucumber line 6457, Horticulture Research, 2025

hifiasm/verkko/hicanu → yahs/allhic (Hi-C) → busco/merqury
  → EDTA/RepeatMasker (TE library) → Augustus/GeneMark/StringTie (RNA-seq-guided)
  → eggNOG/KEGG/GO functional annotation
  → OrthoFinder → RAxML/IQ-TREE species tree → PAML/MCMCTree dating
  → MCScanX/WGDI/JCVI: 1,860 syntenic blocks, Ks peak 0.255 ± 0.034, two WGD events
  → subgenome assignment → CAFE5 (3,086 families expanded at the Populus–Salix node)
  → GO enrichment OF THE EXPANDED FAMILIES → biology

The Populus paper's enriched terms — "lactate biosynthetic process," "glyoxalase III activity," "methylglyoxal catabolic process" — point toward pathogen defence signalling, which is the biological claim the whole trace was built to support.

Lablab adds manual Hi-C curation (Juicer plus Juicebox visual correction of misjoins) and validates with Merqury, CRAQ and BUSCO together. Cucumber demonstrates the terminal move: WGDI subgenome partitions → WGD context → BSA/QTL → candidate gene for pedicel length.

Three meta-lessons. Every stage's output is validated by the next — annotation quality feeds orthology, which feeds family-size calls. The "expanded family → GO enrichment → candidate process" step is where the paper's biology actually happens. And QC is multi-metric because each tool catches different error classes; the plant pangenome white paper (F1000Research, 2025) now makes QUAST-versus-CRAQ reconciliation an explicit recommendation, advising that CRAQ be prioritised for polyploids and cross-validated against raw read alignments.

For a project starting today, substitute HapHiC for YaHS/ALLHiC on a novel plant genome, and add panEDTA if more than one accession is involved.

6.4 The polishing decision pipeline

Luan et al., BMC Genomics, 2024

nanopore reads → Flye OR Unicycler → medaka/Racon (long-read polish)
  → POLCA/Pilon/ntEdit/Polypolish/NextPolish (short-read polish) → compare vs references

Findings that should change practice: Unicycler plus medaka achieved the highest accuracy (median 99.96%, 1,916 nucleotide errors); medaka consistently outperformed Racon (median errors 2,963 versus 9,517); long-read polishing introduced errors into Unicycler assemblies, which already polish internally; short-read polishing rescued Flye assemblies; and homopolymers dominated residual error (96% of Flye errors, 67% of Unicycler errors).

The generalisable lesson is the study design rather than the specific result. A small controlled comparison on your own data, reported as a table, is the most persuasive methods section available — and it is precisely what the 200-insect BRAKER study does for annotation.

6.5 Multi-omics integration — factors, not lists

MOFA+, 2020; Lycoris aurea arid stress, Biology, 2026

The MOFA+ pattern: multiple omics layers on shared samples → factor analysis with ARD priors → factors as coordinated multi-layer variation → t-SNE/UMAP on factor space → GSEA on factor weights → association of factors with sample groups.

The Lycoris aurea arid-stress study demonstrates the plant version, and its distinguishing move is worth copying: DESeq2 → clusterProfiler GO/KEGG → then a gene–metabolite–soil correlation network linking expression, metabolites and environmental variables in a single graph. That is a materially different integration move from presenting two enrichment lists side by side. Pipeline details from the published methods: FastQC v0.11.9 → MultiQC v1.14 → BLASTx (BLAST+ v2.13.0, E ≤ 1e-5) → clusterProfiler v4.6.0 at q ≤ 0.05. Drought-activated GO terms included root hair initiation, cutin biosynthesis, L-proline biosynthesis and malate metabolism — coherent with osmotic adjustment and cuticular reinforcement.

PART VI · WHAT THE EVIDENCE SAYS DOES NOT WORK

7. Honest verdicts

The most useful citations in any field guide are the ones establishing that the more sophisticated option does not win. These are worth knowing before committing resources.

Deep learning does not beat linear baselines for gene perturbation prediction. Nature Methods, 2025, 10.1038/s41592-025-02772-6. Directly relevant to any claim that foundation models have solved functional genomics.

Structural variant caller rankings are an artifact of benchmarking choices. Denti, Krannich, Vinar, Chikhi, Bonizzoni, Brejova & Hormozdiari, bioRxiv, 2025, 10.1101/2025.08.28.672546 (preprint). Across seven callers, rankings flip depending on which assembly-based truth set is used — dipcall versus SVIM-asm versus hapdiff — derived from the very same assemblies. Mean F1 drops from 81% on GRCh37 to 62% on T2T-CHM13, and no universal best performer exists. Counter-intuitively, a more complete reference does not fix the benchmarking problem.

SV calling degrades in low-complexity regions — that is, in exactly the sequence that plant genomes are largely composed of. arXiv:2509.23057, 2025.

Annotation quality dominates orthology results, and different orthology algorithms converge anyway. Bioinformatics 41(7):btaf365, 2025; and PMID 39906489. The Quest for Orthologs benchmark service reports a standing precision–recall tradeoff with no universal winner (NAR Genomics and Bioinformatics 6(4):lqae167, 2024).

Deep learning versus GBLUP for genomic selection is a wash. Montesinos-López et al., Frontiers in Genetics 16:1568705, 2025, 10.3389/fgene.2025.1568705. Deep learning is sometimes better — notably on smaller datasets and for selecting top genotypes — but "neither method consistently outperformed the other across all evaluated traits and scenarios." Given GBLUP's cost and interpretability advantages, it remains the honest default.

DNA language model embeddings frequently fail to beat task-specific supervised baselines. BEND, arXiv:2311.12570. And task-specific self-pretraining rivals foundation-scale pretraining at a fraction of the cost (arXiv:2506.17766) — a quiet indictment of the foundation-model premise.

AlphaGenome improves personal expression prediction but still fails to capture inter-individual variation well. bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.08.05.668750 (preprint). Cite the critique alongside the model.

Standard ORA and GSEA null models are miscalibrated — random gene lists yield significant GO terms. GOAT, Communications Biology, 2024, 10.1038/s42003-024-06454-5.

LLM agents reach only about 0.4 result consistency on real single-cell analysis tasks. Genome Biology, 2026, 10.1186/s13059-026-03998-z, evaluating 8 frontier models across 3 agent frameworks on 50 real tasks. The landscape review (Zhou et al., Briefings in Bioinformatics 26(5):bbaf505, 2025, 10.1093/bib/bbaf505) catalogues roughly fifteen agentic systems and states plainly that "most systems lack rigorous comparative benchmarking against traditional methods," documenting hallucinated API calls, installation failures, memory blowup and prompt sensitivity. The defensible position: viable for boilerplate orchestration under human review, not for unsupervised analysis or interpretation.

Published workflows often do not re-execute. Grayson et al., ACM REP '23, 10.1145/3589806.3600037, on the Snakemake Workflow Catalog and nf-core registries. This is a result in itself, and an argument for containerised, versioned workflows deposited with DOIs.

MAKER2 consistently lags BRAKER3, TOGA and StringTie. Freedman et al., Genome Research, 2025.

PART VII · A WORKED CASE

8. Planning a very large plant genome: Lycoris aurea

Lycoris aurea, the golden spider lily, is a useful worked example because it exercises nearly every difficulty discussed above: an enormous genome, an aneuploid species complex, no usable reference, and a scientifically valuable but crowded research area.

8.1 Genome size and what it implies

Flow cytometry places the L. aurea nuclear genome at 24.05 Gb (Zhang et al., "New insights into interspecies relationships, chromosomal evolution, and hybrid identification in the Lycoris Herb," BMC Plant Biology 25:78, 2025, 10.1186/s12870-025-06112-w), measured against Cycas panzhihuaensis (10.5 Gb) as internal standard. This is the largest monoploid value in the genus. An independent survey of 64 accessions places five L. aurea accessions at 24.88–25.19 Gb (Shu et al., Plant Diversity 47(6):931–943, 2025, 10.1016/j.pld.2025.06.010), which also revises the genus range to 18.03 Gb (L. wulingensis) to 32.62 Gb (L. caldwellii).

At ~25 Gb, 30× HiFi coverage requires roughly 750 Gb of sequence, and the memory table in Section 3.12 puts hifiasm in the 500–800 GB range with several terabytes of working disk.

The Lilium sargentiae precedent (Section 2.6) sets realistic expectations for the shape of the result: a k-mer estimate of 35.66 Gb and a flow cytometry estimate of 31.8 Gb yielded a 47.09 Gb assembly — 32% larger than estimated — driven by 3.35% heterozygosity, with contig N50 of only 0.93 Mb against scaffold N50 of 2.85 Gb, 35 scaffolds and BUSCO 90.8%. Three lessons transfer: plan haplotype handling from the start; do not treat assembly span well above the C-value as a failure signal; and expect the Hi-C library, not the HiFi library, to be doing the structural work — approximately a terabyte of Hi-C, not a token run.

8.2 The karyotype is a hypothesis, not a parameter

L. aurea is frequently described as 2n = 14 = 8M + 6T with x = 7, and Zhang et al. characterise it as "a more symmetrical karyotype, which may be the plesiomorphic state" for the genus.

But it is an aneuploid species complex. Wang et al. (Frontiers in Plant Science 13:955724, 2022), surveying 264 bulbs across 46 populations, report four cytotypes: 2n = 12 (10m+2T), 14 (8m+6T), 15 (7m+8T), and 16 (6m+10T). Shu et al. add 2n = 18 records. Genus base numbers are x = 6, 7, 8 and 11.

The practical consequence is direct: any Phase 6 gate reading "expect 14 chromosome-scale scaffolds" is a one-in-four bet on the provenance of the material. Karyotype and flow-cytometer the specific accession you sequence, retain a voucher, and treat the scaffold count as a measurement rather than a target. Note also that Shu et al. synonymise L. longifolia under L. aurea, so material under that name is in scope.

L. radiata, the obvious comparison species, is similarly complex — a diploid–triploid–tetraploid series, with Liu et al. (Ecology and Evolution 9:6849, 2019; 2,420 individuals across 114 populations) reporting 45.6% diploid populations (2n=22), 50.9% triploid (2n=33), and one tetraploid.

8.3 The reference situation

The first chromosome-level genome for the subfamily Amaryllidoideae was published in June 2026: Zhao Y, Zeng P, Lei P, et al., "A chromosome-level genome assembly of Lycoris radiata reveals the evolutionary origin of Amaryllidaceae alkaloids and elucidates the complete galanthamine biosynthetic pathway," Plant Communications, 10.1016/j.xplc.2026.101942 (PMID 42249611), from Northwestern Polytechnical University with Hubei University of Technology and collaborators.

As of August 2026 the assembly is not retrievable from public archives: NCBI Datasets returns zero genome assemblies for the entire genus Lycoris, and ENA returns zero analysis records. CNCB GWH is the most likely repository given the authors' affiliations but could not be searched programmatically. The only other Amaryllidoideae assembly in NCBI is GCA_032191725.1 (Narcissus pseudonarcissus, 2023) — scaffold-level, 1.15 Gb, with a contig N50 of 332 bp, which is unusable as a reference.

Any plan step depending on reference-guided scaffolding, synteny validation or annotation transfer from L. radiata is therefore blocked until the data appears or is obtained from the authors directly. This is a common situation with recently published genomes and is worth checking early rather than discovering at Phase 6.

8.4 Existing public data

Substantial data already exists and should be inventoried before generating more:

  • PacBio HiFi and Hi-C under BioProject PRJNA1390464 — two Sequel IIe CCS runs (SRR37453806, SRR37453807) plus an Illumina Hi-C run (SRR39719281). The HiFi total is on the order of 1 Tb, which would be roughly 40× for a 25 Gb genome. The larger run's reported base count is anomalously high for a single Sequel IIe run and should be verified against read counts before capacity planning, and the Hi-C run's reported base count requires checking.
  • Iso-Seq: SRR28858249 and SRR28858250, L. aurea full-length cDNA on PacBio Sequel, BioProject PRJNA1106651, roughly 42 Gb combined — tier 1 annotation evidence, already available.
  • Short-read RNA-seq: approximately 72 public runs across tissues and treatments (PRJNA1065507, PRJNA1106235, PRJNA1328393, PRJNA1100760).
  • Assembled transcriptomes: AmarylOmicBase (dos Santos, Mérindol & Desgagné-Penix, bioRxiv 2025.11.24.690262, preprint) ships two L. aurea assemblies — Lycoris_aurea_PB (PacBio-only, TSA DBNFTY000000000) and Lycoris_aurea_TH (Trinity hybrid, TSA DBNKRI000000000) — alongside L. radiata, L. chinensis, L. incarnata, L. longituba and L. sprengeri.
  • Organellar: the L. aurea plastome is MN831471 (158,690 bp; Peng, Wei & Yang, Mitochondrial DNA Part B 5(1):788–789, 2020). The L. radiata plastome is MN158120 (RefSeq NC_045077, 158,335 bp; Zhang et al., Mitochondrial DNA Part B 4(2):2886–2887, 2019). Note that two L. aurea plastomes exist and disagree on gene count (MN831471 at 127 genes versus MN158985 at 137), and the published partition figures for MN831471 do not sum to its stated total — re-annotate from your own assembly rather than inheriting either.
  • Markers: 839 non-redundant EST-SSRs from 59,643 unigenes (Jiang et al., BMC Plant Biology 20:522, 2020); 17 microsatellites (Chen et al., Applications in Plant Sciences 6(11):e01198, 2018); 45S and 5S rDNA FISH mapping (Chang, Shii & Chung, JASHS 134(5):567–573, 2009; Liu et al., PLoS ONE 16(9):e0258028, 2021).

No complete mitochondrial genome exists for any Lycoris species — NCBI holds only four partial gene fragments. A mitogenome falls out of HiFi data essentially for free and is a publishable companion resource.

8.5 What the comparison paper sets as the bar

The expected downstream for a new Lycoris genome is no longer annotation plus CAFE. The L. radiata paper's arc is:

chromosome-level assembly
  → comparative genomics (ancestral angiosperm origin of core Amaryllidaceae alkaloid genes;
     complete pathway assembled in Amaryllidoideae via duplication + neofunctionalisation)
  → integrated metabolomics + transcriptomics (roots actively contribute to alkaloid metabolism)
  → heterologous reconstruction of the galanthamine pathway in Yarrowia lipolytica

That is the full assembly → gene map → pathway → engineering arc, and it is now the reviewer's reference point. A second 2026 paper covers overlapping ground (Yuan et al., "Deciphering the galantamine biosynthetic pathway in Lycoris species," Plant Physiology and Biochemistry, 10.1016/j.plaphy.2026.111252), so the pathway itself is crowded.

The novelty case for an L. aurea assembly is therefore not the assembly but the comparison: synteny against radiata, alkaloid gene-family dynamics, selection on biosynthetic clusters, and — most distinctively — the aneuploid series as a natural experiment. Because L. aurea spans 2n = 12 to 18 at essentially constant genome size (24.88–25.19 Gb across cytotypes), the chromosome-number variation is close to pure fusion and fission rather than gain or loss of sequence. Assembling two cytotypes and comparing breakpoints against centromere position (TRASH/HiCAT plus CENH3 ChIP) would be a clean test of centromere-drive versus Robertsonian mechanisms, and there is no comparable system with this much karyotypic range at constant genome size.

Recent literature for context: the current pathway review is "Navigating Amaryllidaceae alkaloids: bridging gaps and charting biosynthetic territories," Journal of Experimental Botany 76(1):16–34, 2025, 10.1093/jxb/erae187. Family-level phylogenomics: Dennehy-Carr et al., American Journal of Botany, 2025, 10.1002/ajb2.70092, dating the Amaryllidaceae divergence to southern Africa around 48.6 Mya.

9. Open opportunities

Ten gaps visible across the reviewed literature, offered as tractable projects rather than speculation.

  1. Presence/absence × expression interaction analysis. Pangenome papers report PAV; transcriptome papers report expression; almost nobody tests them jointly. A gene absent from a sample's pangenome path is invisible to DESeq2, which silently treats structural absence as zero expression. The tools exist (vg, GraphAligner, rpvg); the workflow does not.

  2. Annotation transfer as a quality metric. Liftoff-style transfer is used as a convenience. It could be a validation: transfer a curated annotation from a well-studied relative, compare against the ab initio prediction, and quantify the disagreement as an assembly and annotation quality score. OMArk and PSAURON now provide two orthogonal axes to calibrate such a score against.

  3. WGD-aware gene family birth-and-death analysis. CAFE5 operates on gene trees; WGDI operates on syntenic blocks. Few analyses condition CAFE5 on WGD block assignment, despite WGD-derived and small-scale duplicates having different evolutionary dynamics. With ksrates correcting rate heterogeneity in the Ks step, this conditioning is now defensible in a way it was not several years ago.

  4. Multi-omics factors tested against phenotypes. MOFA+ factors are typically described rather than tested. Factor loadings used as pseudo-phenotypes in association analysis would connect multi-omics integration directly to genetic architecture.

  5. Benchmark-first pipeline design. Before running the analysis, run a small controlled comparison of tool choices on your own data and report the comparison, not just the winner. This converts "we used X" into "we established X is appropriate here."

  6. Graph-based variant calling in non-model plants. Human genomics has pangenome graphs and graph-aware callers; plant work still largely calls against a single linear reference. Species complexes with high chromosomal variation are a harder and more interesting version of the problem.

  7. Aneuploid series as natural experiments. Where chromosome number varies at constant genome size, fusion and fission breakpoints can be compared directly against centromere position, testing mechanistic hypotheses that are otherwise inaccessible.

  8. Methylome-from-assembly-reads as a standard deliverable. Every HiFi and ONT plant genome paper already contains the methylation calls in its raw data and discards them. Publishing the assembly and a CHG/CHH/CpG methylome at no additional sequencing cost should be the default; the obstacle is partly tooling defaults and partly convention.

  9. Pan-TE-corrected reanalysis of published pangenomes. Given that panEDTA exists and per-genome TE libraries demonstrably corrupt PAV calls, reanalysing published crop pangenomes with a unified pan-TE library would quantify how much reported presence/absence variation is annotation artifact. Precedent exists: in bacterial pangenomics, annotation artifacts account for a large share of apparent gene absences.

  10. Routine proteogenomic annotation QC. Peptides mapping outside annotated CDS are the only non-computational evidence that a gene model is wrong, and MS data for many crop species already exists in PRIDE. Systematic re-searching against new assemblies would produce a genuine annotation error rate — a figure the field currently lacks.

10. Scope, sources and limitations

Scope. Emphasis is on eukaryotic — particularly plant — genome projects using long reads, with human and microbial work included where it establishes method or provides contrast. Clinical genomics, metagenomics and phylogenetics beyond genome-scale dating are out of scope.

Sources. Literature identified via Europe PMC, OpenAlex and Unpaywall across 28 topic patterns; approximately 260 candidate papers ranked by citation and recency, with 18 read in full for pipeline extraction. Tool versions were checked against bioconda and upstream repositories; commands were verified against current official documentation. Limitations. Citation-ranked selection favours canonical tools, so recent work is included deliberately rather than emerging from the ranking. Several 2025–26 items exist only as preprints and are labelled as such in the text; preprint findings have not been peer reviewed and occasionally do not survive review.
